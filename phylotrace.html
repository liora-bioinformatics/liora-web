The challenge

Sequencing has scaled. Analysis hasn’t.

Whole-genome sequencing shows how pathogens spread — through hospitals, food chains and the environment — often long before observation alone would. Many laboratories can now generate genomes routinely. Far fewer can interpret them together with their epidemiological data — without bioinformatics support, they chain several tools, and sharing means handing over data.

deaths associated with 33 bacterial pathogens in 2019 — 13.6 % of all deaths worldwide1

1.27 M

600 M

people — almost 1 in 10 — fall ill each year after eating contaminated food: bacteria, viruses, parasites and chemicals combined3

Genomes leave the lab to be analysed.

Results return late, while an investigation loses value by the day — and epidemiological judgement concentrates in a few institutions.

Comparability needs coordination.

Locally assigned allele numbers drift apart between laboratories unless they are synchronised with a central authority, so every participant depends on it.

Sharing becomes a governance problem.

Pooling data in one place means sensitive metadata — about patients, producers or sites — has to move too.

Experts call for building genomic analysis capacity and training for the people who run surveillance.4 That takes software they can run themselves.

PhyloTrace at a glance

Genomes and epidemiology in one platform.

PhyloTrace is an open-source platform for pathogen surveillance and monitoring. It integrates genomic data with epidemiological metadata, so you can follow what is happening now and look back at what has already happened — in public health, healthcare, food-associated and environmental settings, on your own hardware and without writing code.

1

Genomes and epidemiology together

Typing results, resistance findings and your own metadata — time, place, source, any variable — are analysed side by side in one place.

2

Ongoing and retrospective

Monitor continuously as isolates arrive, or analyse existing collections retrospectively at any time. Saved analyses reopen exactly as you left them.

3

Comparable without a central server

Every allele is identified by a fingerprint of its sequence, so partner laboratories get identical results without coordinating.

4

Verifiable and under your control

Every result keeps its provenance — assembly fingerprint, scheme version, thresholds and tool versions — so it stays explainable years later. GPLv3 open source on your own hardware; you decide field by field what leaves your laboratory.

From routine monitoring to looking back.

PhyloTrace works the same way whether you watch for what is emerging or investigate what has already happened — in public health, healthcare, food-associated and environmental settings.

Surveillance works on two levels: fast local results to recognise and contain outbreaks, and comparison across institutions and countries that supports the search for resistant clones or food-chain problems — in a One Health perspective.5

Inside the platform

Everything in one place.

PhyloTrace covers the whole surveillance routine in five areas and keeps everything together in a single portable file. Work wherever you need — start a new collection, keep an ongoing one up to date, or bring in existing data and look back.

Set up

Choose from 42 schemes covering the most relevant bacterial species, ready to use and with nothing to convert by hand. Support for custom schemes will be added.

Type & screen

Add genomes one at a time or type large batches automatically, whole existing collections included. Each genome is typed and screened for resistance and virulence in a single run.

Curate

Add epidemiological information — wards, specimen or food sources, sampling sites — and any variable you define. Many quality indicators are shown for every isolate.

Analyse

Explore relatedness, time and place with trees, epidemic curves, maps and resistance heatmaps. Save analyses for routine monitoring, or revisit historical collections at any time.

Share

Export any subset of isolates and choose field by field which information travels with them. Partner laboratories get what a collaboration needs while sensitive metadata stays in yours.

One file holds everything.

Genomes, metadata, results and saved analyses all live in one portable file — a standard SQL database that common tools can open, so your data is never locked in. To back up, copy it. To share, send it.

cgMLST and MLST typing

Fast and automated: 253 genomes in 49 minutes on a desktop workstation (see case study). cgMLST gives high resolution; classical MLST runs alongside for comparability with published sequence types. 42 bacterial species schemes are available straight from cgMLST.org. Only intact coding sequences become new alleles — so every comparison starts from clean data and stays comparable over time. Support for custom schemes will be added.

Resistance, virulence and point mutations

Every assembly is screened with NCBI AMRFinderPlus for resistance genes, virulence factors, stress, biocide and metal tolerance, and species-specific resistance mutations. Resistance heatmaps show results by gene, by drug class or as ranked prevalence — so resistance is part of every analysis, not a separate project, and emerging resistance stands out across your collection.

Collection overviews and quality control

Dedicated overviews for isolates, cgMLST, MLST and resistance summarise provenance, assembly quality, scheme and locus information, profile completeness, sequence types and allele diversity, and the settings and versions used for each run. Consistency checks, completeness scores and duplicate checks flag weak data — so you can see exactly what your collection holds and how it was typed, and weak data never distorts clusters.

Collaborate without centralising

Traditional surveillance pools every dataset at a central hub. By design, every participant then depends on that hub: whether it is reachable, how it is configured and what its rules and governance allow. A decentralised network of laboratories — public health, healthcare, food and environmental — reduces that dependence: each laboratory keeps full analytical capability on its own hardware, decides what it shares and compares results directly with its partners.

Alleles are identified by a fingerprint of their sequence, so partner laboratories get identical identifiers without coordinating — no renumbering, no central nomenclature server.1 Exchange a database file (merged only if species and scheme match, with automatic backup and a label showing each isolate’s origin) or a profile table (partner isolates still appear in your trees and networks). You choose field by field which metadata travels — so you share relatedness while patient identifiers stay in your lab.

Each laboratory can keep analysing and comparing whenever a partner or a central service is unavailable — and stays in control of its data.

1 On the dependence on central nomenclature servers in outbreak settings: Deneke et al., Frontiers in Microbiology 12:649517, 2021, doi:10.3389/fmicb.2021.649517.

Networks and trees

Identical profiles are grouped in minimum spanning trees, every edge is labelled with its allelic distance, and clusters are called at the scheme’s published threshold. Neighbour-joining and UPGMA trees come rectangular or circular, with resistance findings beside the tips and any metadata variable mapped onto them — so you see at a glance which isolates belong together, and lineage and resistance are read side by side.

Maps and epidemic curves

Bin cases by day, week, month or year and play epidemic curves and maps forward through time, with interactive overlays of any variable — so you see when and where transmission happened and uncover patterns that might otherwise stay hidden in spreadsheets.

Provenance and reproducibility

Every result keeps its provenance: the assembly fingerprint, scheme version, thresholds and tool versions it was produced with are stored alongside it, and alleles are identified by a hash of their sequence — so results stay explainable years later and can be verified and reproduced, by you or by a partner.

Your metadata, your variables

Standard fields such as date, source and location, plus custom variables of any kind — wards, environmental data, numerical values, susceptibility results. Any variable can be put on any graph (trees, networks, maps, epidemic curves); PhyloTrace picks a fitting colour scheme and encoding — so relatedness is always read in its epidemiological context, and you explore hypotheses instead of formatting plots.

Analysis dashboard

Saved analyses reopen exactly as you left them, flag data changes and export as PDF, SVG or PNG. Routine groups grow with newly added isolates, and subsets or snapshots of the database state make analyses reproducible and shareable — so routine surveillance takes a click, not a project.

Trust & sustainability

Open, local — and here to stay.

Surveillance software is a long-term decision. PhyloTrace is built so that your data stays yours and the software stays available.

Your data, your jurisdiction

Local by design

All analysis runs on your own hardware. Genomes and patient-related metadata leave your laboratory only if you share them.

Data protection

PhyloTrace is designed with data protection in mind: genomes and metadata stay on your own hardware. Liora hosts no customer data, has no permanent access to it and collects no telemetry.

Made in Germany

Developed and supported by Liora Bioinformatics in Rottweil, with research roots at Furtwangen University.

Research use only

PhyloTrace is not a medical device. Laboratories validate results for their own purposes under their own quality system.

Built on established methods

Hash-based allele identity is an established principle (Eyre 2019; Deneke 2021). PhyloTrace builds on pyMLST and AMRFinderPlus and works alongside public platforms.

Open source that lasts

Yours to keep

Licensed under GPLv3 — the source code stays available to you, whatever happens.

Public and archived

Code on GitHub, archived on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.10996423).

Releases & roadmap

Regular maintenance updates and new features. Planned next: plasmid typing, support for cgMLST schemes beyond those currently included, and assembly generation — taking PhyloTrace end to end, from sequencing data to surveillance result.1

Maintenance you can rely on

Open source brings transparency and independence; its risk is that maintenance depends on goodwill. Subscriptions fund it: for subscribers, support for the current and the previous public version.

Academic backing

Developed with Furtwangen University (HFU) and the Medical University of Graz (MUG); manuscript in preparation.
